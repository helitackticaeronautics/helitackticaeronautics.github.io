Our mission

Our mission is to give wildland firefighters new capabilities with purpose built unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)

Current action

We are speaking with firefighting departments, knowledge keepers, provincial agencies, and researchers to learn about current wildfire management capabilities and gaps.

Goal of engagement

Identify where UAVs can help, driven by what stakeholders are asking for and what the current technology can support.

Underlying rational

The climate is changing… We want to empower firefighters to create more resilient communities today and in the future.

Why wildfires are getting worse

Fire seasons are starting earlier, lasting longer, and outpacing the people and aircraft sent to meet them. The gap between when a fire starts and when it's found is where lives, homes, and forests are won or lost.

  • The country is warming fast

    Canada is warming faster than the global average, especially in northern regions. Record heat in recent years is driving hotter, more intense fires that are pushing the limits of existing response capacity.

  • Fire seasons are becoming longer

    Canada's three worst fire seasons on record have all happened recently. Warmer winters are producing shallower snowpacks and earlier springs. Snow melts sooner, landscapes dry out earlier, and fire season starts earlier and runs longer

  • Drought conditions are intensifying

    Extreme heat and drought are becoming more common in all corners of Canada. Drier fuels ignite more easily and burn more severely.

Sources: Standing Senate Committee on Agriculture and Forestry. (2026, June). Canada on fire: The catastrophic and escalating effects of wildfires on lives and communities. Senate of Canada. https://sencanada.ca
